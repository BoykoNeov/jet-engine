Slice S (rungs 43/45, `TwoSpoolFuelTransient`) pre-registered 2026-08-18 as § 5.16 of
`docs/plans/todo-rust-port.md`. Four probes measured first. Two process lessons, both about
reasoning that LOOKED measured.

**An inherited IOU can name a gas the code refuses.** [[rust-port-phase6-preflight]] booked slice S
"the arming-predicate measurement on the reacting gas", and [[rust-port-slice-r-step4]] narrowed it
to "on its own object". But the fuel path asserts against an equilibrium gas outright, so the exact
gas the detector had fired on was unreachable. Re-pointing it at the three gases that ARE admitted
turned a would-be dropped line into the slice's leading result. **Before discharging an inherited
IOU, check the object it names is reachable — and if it is not, say so as a finding rather than
quietly substituting a different measurement.**

**"Did not move" is only a discharge if the thing was ever at risk**, and my first margin probe
measured the wrong population — it timed the predicate's threshold across every solver iteration,
and those converge ONTO the threshold by construction, so it reported a margin of zero when the
real one is seven orders. **Locate a branch site exactly before measuring its margin; a solver's
own evaluations are not the predicate's.**

**And my prediction 4 repeated, verbatim, the reasoning that killed the neighbouring slice's
prediction 8** — "the values will see it too, because these are maxima over the whole trajectory".
The advisor caught it; measuring took one probe and refuted it 12 cells out of 12. **When a
prediction's justification matches one the plan has already recorded as refuted, that is a reason
to measure it, not to write it.**
