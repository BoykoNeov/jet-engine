**PER-ROW BLADING (rung 56's named seam) — investigated 2026-07-28, NEGATIVE, NOT a rung.**
`docs/per-row-blading-negative.md`. Precedent class: [[mixing-scale-negative]],
[[mixing-jicf-anchor-negative]] — killed for riding on an unanchored knob.

**The seam was wrong about WHAT is missing.** Rung 56 said the ladder does not supply a
`ψ_k`/`T_c,k` law. It supplies both, with zero new constants: at design `β₁`=45° for every row,
so `Δh_k = U²(1−t₂,k)` derives the per-row metal angle, the work split, `l_k`, and — via a
row-invariant critical DIFFUSION ratio anchored on rung 36's `φ_surge` — `T_c,k`. Even the
taper's ceiling is anchored (de Haller 0.72 ⇒ `t_max` 0.534/0.616). What the ladder does NOT
supply is **the ANCHOR — what is held fixed while the blades change** — and every sign and
magnitude answers to it, not to the blading.

**The real finding is an IMPOSSIBILITY, and it is provable.** Pin the machine
(`Σ(1−t₂,k)` fixed ⇒ `U` fixed) *and* pin the front blade, and a monotone
front-low-turning/rear-high-turning stack **cannot exist**: the mean over rows ≥1 must equal
`t₂,ref`, so a decreasing sequence must start above it ⇒ row 1 turns LESS than row 0.
`K`-independent. **The seam's request is over-determined** — which is exactly why each anchor
gave a different verdict (each drops a different one of the three requirements).

**Verdict on the well-posed anchor** (decision rule fixed before reading): capacity **inert**
(max 1.61 %, non-monotone), incidence +0.6…4.6 % and purely positional. Rung 56's headline is
**consistent with, not overturned by**, the obvious second lever — stated no more strongly,
because only one anchor family was tested and its well-posed member has a non-monotone shape.

**By-product, and it stands alone: CORRECTS rung 55.** Its work split was framed as free
robustness furniture; inverted, a split IS a blading law. At the shipped design point `dT` ⇔
taper 0 (uniform blades) and `tau` ⇔ taper 0.50 LP / 0.64 HP, with the HP **violating de
Haller** (0.7185 < 0.72). Verdicts survive; the framing does not. Also anchor-independent:
`U²(t)/U²(0) = S(0)/S(t)` — a one-row-anchored taper does not preserve the machine, it sets the
design blade speed (±29 % over the range probed).

**Two process lessons, both mine to keep.** (1) I nearly shipped a five-times-restated
"coordinate-dependent SIGN" headline built on a `C`→0.99 endpoint that `with_capacity`'s own
assertion would refuse — the advisor's blocking mean-anchor probe killed it. (2) I then quoted
+5…18 % incidence referenced to an endpoint whose binding row had itself moved —
[[rung43-two-shaft-fuel-metering]]'s currency-circularity trap, caught again by the advisor.
Re-referenced to the uniform point it
is +0.6…4.6 %, and the negative got *stronger*.
