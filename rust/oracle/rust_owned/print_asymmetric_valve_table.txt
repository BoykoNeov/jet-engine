  Each valve is priced by ONE edge. The incidence valve's set point is its OPENING speed's:
  its margin bottoms out before it ever closes, so how fast it closes only adds or saves the
  air it holds on the way down. The phi valve's margin bottoms out AFTER it starts closing, so
  its set point is its CLOSING speed's (0.3805-0.3812 down the 0.01 column, whatever the
  opening) - until the closing is slow enough that the minimum comes first, and it sits on its
  floor. (At an opening of 0.2 the symmetric valve's minimum already comes first: its 'floor'
  is its own set point.) So the closing speed moves the verdict BOTH ways: a slow close makes
  the phi valve the cheaper one at every opening speed; a fast close hands the incidence valve
  the slow openings too. Over the 19 scored cells (spec section 3) the incidence valve is cheaper
  in 68 of 95 runs closing at 0.01 and in 0 of 95 closing at 0.2 - against 54 and 11 of 95 when
  the OPENING is held at 0.01 or 0.2: the closing speed is the stronger dial.
