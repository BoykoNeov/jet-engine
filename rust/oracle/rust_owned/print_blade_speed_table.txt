
THE BLADE-SPEED WALLS (rung 85): the stack gets a physical blade speed, and the stage
count K, the capacity C and a REDLINE R = N_red/N_d become OUTPUTS. Two walls per spool:
an AIRFLOW level (front-row relative tip Mach -- an efficiency target that SIZES the design)
and a STRENGTH wall (blade-root pull, Ti-6Al-4V AMS 4911 min 827.4 MPa / 4429 kg/m^3,
with 14 CFR 33.27's 120 % overspeed) -- the only OFF-design wall. Design flow coefficient
Phi_d = 0.537 (NASA Rotor 37's mean line).

  THE ROUNDING KNOB lambda: K must be whole, so K = ceil(K*) leaves a gap below the wall.
  lambda = 0 slows the blades to close it (rungs 55/56's machine); lambda = 1 keeps them AT
  the wall and loads every row lighter (r = its work vs the unswirled row) through a design
  pre-swirl -- exactly a reshaped map: l' = (1+l)/r - 1, sigma' = sigma/r (droop switch A).

  WHAT IT SAYS:
  * Holding the design incidence pins 1/phi - v = 1, and then psi = 1 - sigma u^2 - l u - v(1+l)phi
    collapses to 1 + u - sigma u^2: on the lumped machine the held speed carries NO map slope l.
    So lambda reaches a held schedule only through the droop sigma -- switch A (droop tied to
    blade speed) raises it, switch B (droop shrinks with row work) leaves it within 0.1 %.
  * lambda = 1 lowers every redline (faster blades); on switch A the h=0.5 M=1.5 cell's lumped
    LP schedule then crosses its redline, by under 0.1 %.
  * The all-rows LP schedule never reaches its target at Tt4 = 1100/1000 and passes the redline
    on the way (V1); the HP all-rows one stays 1.6 % under the tightest HP redline.
  * The front-row lever raises N_L but LOWERS the front row's tip Mach (a reading, never a wall).
