
A BURNER THAT CARRIES THE LOAD (rung 94): each throttle holds the thrust - or the HP speed - this engine
gives there with the generator off, and the burner temperature rises to carry the generator. Two-spool
engine, pi 3 x 6, flat maps; the generator a fixed power (x the design HP compressor power).

  1. The burner's rise, and each compressor's flow coefficient against the same load at a fixed burner
     temperature (+ is away from stall). Thrust and speed held against a fixed temperature.

  2. Holding the 700 K thrust: what a load needs, and what it cannot have.
  HP 0.10: the burner at 983.84 K (the least it can run at: 973.99 K)
  LP 0.05: The thrust asked for (88.8000 N per kg/s of design airflow) is below the least this load allows: the engine needs at least 913 K to carry it at all, and gives 142.6912 N per kg/s of design airflow there.

  Carrying the generator, the hotter burner pushes more air: the LP compressor sits further from stall than at a
  fixed burner temperature at every power, and an LP-shaft generator leaves BOTH compressors further from stall
  than no generator at all, above idle. Holding HP speed is not holding thrust: on the HP shaft it burns hotter and
  gives more thrust; on the LP shaft it can run the burner cooler. A load near a throttle's fold can still be held
  if the burner may rise - but an LP-shaft load lifts the thrust the engine can run at, and below it no hold exists.
