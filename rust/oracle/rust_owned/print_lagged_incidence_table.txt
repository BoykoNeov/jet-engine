
THE SLOW VALVE ON A MOVING TARGET (rung 88): rung 87's bleed valve beside the LP stator
schedule, given rung 65's lag tau (the valve chases its instant opening). Shaped maps,
r = 0.5, set point sm = 0.42 (the incidence margin m_lim it is asked to hold).

  1. The incidence valve, made slow. Its bleed bill is tau x the opening it starts with
     (here 0: the stators protect the start) plus a small drift; its protection falls.

     tau M_i - m_lim        b(0) extra bleed       drift
    0.01   -3.656e-3     0.000e0    9.413e-6    9.002e-6
    0.05   -1.273e-2     0.000e0    4.335e-5    4.291e-5
    0.20   -2.580e-2     0.000e0    1.062e-4    1.141e-4

  2. Both valves re-tuned, at each speed, to hold the SAME incidence margin m_lim:

     tau    inc sm   inc bleed    phi sm   phi bleed    ratio        dF
    inst    0.4200   9.4847e-3    0.3447   2.1596e-2    2.277    -1.594
    0.01    0.4243   1.0863e-2    0.3384   1.9071e-2    1.756    -1.064
    0.05    0.4376   1.5544e-2    0.3205   1.1873e-2    0.764     0.374
    0.20    0.4657   2.7880e-2    0.3355   1.9587e-2    0.703     0.871

  ratio = phi valve's bleed / incidence valve's; dF = phi's thrust integral minus incidence's.
  Instant, the incidence valve is the cheaper one (rung 87). Slow enough, the order REVERSES:
  the incidence valve waits until the margin is needed and then must open fast, so a lag costs
  it protection it has to buy back; the phi valve opens early -- the stators lower phi from the
  start -- and that head start absorbs the lag. Where the crossover sits in tau is a measurement
  on this rig's own time scale, not a statement about real valves.
